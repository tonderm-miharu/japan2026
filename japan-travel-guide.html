Culture & Tradition

The Art of Onsen

Japan's sacred bathing culture across volcanic landscapes

Japan sits atop the Pacific Ring of Fire, blessed with over 27,000 natural hot springs. The onsen tradition dates back to the 8th century, creating sanctuaries of relaxation where social barriers dissolve along with one's clothes.

Beppu - Hell on Earth

With eight distinct "hells" and more hot spring sources than anywhere else in Japan, Beppu produces enough hot water to fill 100 Olympic pools daily. Steam rises from streets, cooking pits bubble with geothermal heat.

Kurokawa Onsen

Hidden in Kumamoto's mountains, this intimate village offers the tegata system - a wooden pass granting access to three different ryokan baths. Lantern-lit paths connect traditional inns with cave baths, riverside rotenburo, and forest-view pools.

"In an onsen, rank and status dissolve in the steam. A CEO soaks beside a student. The water makes us equal."

Onsen Etiquette

  • Wash thoroughly before entering
  • Never wear swimwear (unless specified)
  • Keep towel out of the water
  • Don't swim or splash
  • Tattoos may restrict access
  • Limit soaking to 15-20 minutes

Adventure

Shimanami Kaido

70km across the Seto Inland Sea - Japan's most spectacular cycling route

Connecting Honshu to Shikoku across six islands, the Shimanami Kaido is more than a bridge - it's a journey through maritime history, artistic villages, and breathtaking coastal scenery.

The Route

Starting from Onomichi, the route crosses Mukaishima, Innoshima, Ikuchijima, Omishima, Hakatajima, and Oshima before reaching Imabari. Each island offers citrus groves, ancient shrines, contemporary art, and fishing villages where time moves slowly.

Ikuchijima: Art & History

Home to Kosanji Temple - a millionaire's tribute featuring replicas of famous buildings - and the stunning Hill of Hope with marble sculptures overlooking the sea.

Omishima: Sacred Island

Oyamazumi Shrine has stood for over 2,600 years, housing Japan's largest samurai armor collection. Stay at SANDO, a modern guesthouse bridging tradition and contemporary design.

Practical Tips

  • Rent bikes in Onomichi (Giant Store)
  • Reserve 2 days for full experience
  • Follow blue line painted on road
  • Ship luggage ahead via Yamato
  • Don't miss: Salt ice cream on Hakatajima
  • Kirosan Observatory: Kengo Kuma, steep 3km climb

Alternative Adventure

Aogashima

Japan's most remote volcanic caldera - the hardest place to reach

A volcanic island within a volcanic island, inhabited by ~170 people, accessible only by helicopter (when weather permits) or a ferry that cancels more often than it runs.

Getting There: The Challenge

The 9-seat helicopter books out within minutes. Tickets go on sale exactly one month in advance at 9:00 AM Japan time. The ferry cancels ~40% of attempts in autumn.

What Awaits

Oyama Viewpoint: Views into the double caldera that seem from another planet

Maruyama Crater: 40-minute hike into the inner volcano with steam vents

Sauna Jigoku: Natural geothermal sauna built into rock - free, primitive

Stargazing: Zero light pollution, Milky Way visible to naked eye
